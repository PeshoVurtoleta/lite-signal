What this is: a local JavaScript port of the community reactivity benchmark
(js-reactivity-benchmark, transitive-bullshit's fork of milomg's "reactively"
benchmark). It is the cross-framework PERFORMANCE suite -- kairo propagation
shapes, the `mol` bench, the SolidJS `S` create/update micro-suite, the `cellx`
fan, and the `dyn` dynamic-component topologies. It complements benchmark.mjs
(lite-signal's own anti-DCE throughput harness in results.txt). Each engine runs
in its own cold process (FW=<engine>), so there is no cross-engine inline-cache
pollution or warm-heap borrowing.

1.3.0 -> 1.4.0 IS DROP-IN on this suite. The hot paths are byte-identical; 1.4.0
adds three cumulative counters to `stats()` (totalAllocations, totalDisposals,
poolGrowths) that bump on the existing acquire / dispose / pool-grow edges only.
The numbers move within run-to-run noise of the 1.3.0 run; the construction rows
(S: createComputations*) show host-noise variance but no architectural change.
(prealloc: "lazy" trades a little first-construction allocation for a smaller
resident heap and faster cold start; steady state is identical.)

Methodology note -- why median, not mean, and why isolated:
  On this 10-year-old 4-core host a single run occasionally spikes from a GC pause;
  median-of-10 removes that. Each engine runs in its own cold process so no engine
  pollutes another's inline caches or borrows its warm heap. The 10 raw runs sit
  under bench/bench-runs-reactive/run_1.txt ... run_10.txt and can be re-medianed
  independently.

VersionMatrix gate (harness/VersionMatrix/): the same-host cold-process regression
  gate that runs on prepublish. Four workloads map to the shapes above --
  reactive-graph-mix (kairo + mol pattern), deep-chain (kairo: deep / cellx pull),
  broadcast-fanout (BROADCAST from the propagation bench), and dynamic-dep-churn
  (dyn: dynamic / SELECTIVE DAG family). A candidate must clear both a fixed floor
  (frame.avg <=10%, p99 <=30% vs the never-regress baseline) AND the rolling
  baseline (frame.avg <=5%, p99 <=18% vs the previous published version); tighter
  on the anchor metric, generous on the jitter-prone p99s. Details in
  harness/VersionMatrix/README.md.

Note: times from this host are a relative baseline; run on a quiet machine for
publishable absolute numbers. The relative ordering between libraries is what
matters. Prior baselines (1.3.0, 1.2.2, 1.2.0, 1.1.5) are in git history.
